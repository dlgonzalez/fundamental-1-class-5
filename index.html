Nouns & Articles (Deep Dive)

A noun is a word that names a Person, Place, Thing, or Animal. Let's master how to say "one" thing (A/An) and how to say "many" things (Plurals) in real-world contexts.

Grammar in Context: Why do we use them?

Function Example in Action
1. Describing Professions I am a teacher. She is an engineer.
2. Identifying Objects (Singular) It is an apple. I need a phone.
3. Describing Quantities (Plural) I have three cars. I see two women.
Common Mistake: Do NOT use A/An with plural nouns!
❌ I see a cats.    ✅ I see cats.

Rule 1: A vs. An (Singular)

A Consonant Sound

Use "A" before a word that starts with a consonant sound.

Example: I am a teacher.

An Vowel Sound

Use "An" before a word that starts with a vowel sound.

Example: I am an engineer.

Rule 2: Singular to Plural

1. Most Nouns: + s

Car ➔ Cars

Dog ➔ Dogs

2. Ends in s, sh, ch, x: + es

Bus ➔ Buses

Watch ➔ Watches

3. Consonant + y: -ies

City ➔ Cities

Baby ➔ Babies

4. Irregulars ⚠️

Man ➔ Men

Child ➔ Children

Person ➔ People

The Verb To Be

The verb To Be (am, is, are) is the engine of English. We use it to describe people and things. Let's master the Affirmative and Negative forms in complex sentences.

Subject Pronouns & Nouns

In English, sentences need a Subject. You can use a Name/Noun (Maria, The dog) OR a Pronoun (She, It). Pronouns replace nouns so we don't repeat them!

He1 Man / Boy
(e.g., Tom, My brother)
She1 Woman / Girl
(e.g., Maria, My sister)
It1 Thing / Animal
(e.g., The car, The dog)
They2+ People/Things
(e.g., Tom & Maria, Dogs)
WeAnother person + I
(e.g., Tom and I)
YouThe person I am talking to
(Tú / Ustedes)
